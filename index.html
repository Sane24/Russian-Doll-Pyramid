1. The plate

Each scan is one tall grey image with three exposures stacked top to bottom: blue, green, red. I cut it into thirds, keep blue fixed, shift green and red until they line up with blue, and stack the three as RGB. Every offset on this page is (x, y) in pixels, the amount a channel was moved to land on blue.

2. Single-scale alignment

For the small JPEGs I try every shift (dx, dy) with both coordinates in [−15, 15], roll the channel by it with np.roll, and score it against blue with normalized cross-correlation: subtract each image's mean, divide by the norms, take the dot product. Higher is better. I only score the middle 80% of the frame, so plate borders and wrapped-around pixels don't get a vote. That's 31 × 31 = 961 candidates per channel.

NCC(a, b) = ⟨a − ā, b − b̄⟩ / ( ‖a − ā‖ · ‖b − b̄‖ )

3. Image pyramid

The TIFFs are about 3700 × 3200 px per channel and the shifts reach 177 px, so searching at full resolution is out. Instead I halve the image (anti-aliased rescale) until its longest side is under 400 px, run the plain ±15 search there, and hand the answer back up: at each level, double the previous estimate, then check only ±2 px around it. Emir gets five levels, 1/16 to full. The whole run costs 961 + 4 × 25 correlations instead of tens of thousands.

4. Emir, and why raw pixels fail

With NCC on raw pixels the pyramid works on ten of the eleven TIFFs. On Emir, green lands at (24, 49) but red goes to (−205, 141). The robe is bright under the blue filter and dark under the red one, so at the correct alignment the two channels nearly anti-correlate and a wrong shift scores better. The pyramid can't fix that: the 1/16 level already picks (−13, 9), and every level above just doubles it. Raw NCC scored 0.71 for green and 0.28 for red, which was the warning sign.

Sobel edges instead of pixels

Sobel gives gradient magnitude, which is the same whether an edge goes light-to-dark or dark-to-light. So I run sobel() on each channel, run the identical pyramid on the edge maps, and apply the resulting shifts to the original channels. Emir's red moves to (40, 107). On every other plate the offsets change by at most 4 px, which is why I hadn't noticed the problem earlier. All TIFF results below use this version.

5. Automatic cropping

np.roll wraps the rows it pushes off one edge back in at the other, and the scans have thick borders. Two steps: first keep only the rectangle all three shifted channels cover (for Harvesters, with G at (17, 60) and R at (14, 124), that's right of column 17 and below row 124). Then convert to grey, run Sobel, average edge strength along every row and column, and in the outer 15% of each side cut just inside the strongest line, with a 1% margin. If that would remove more than 40% of either dimension, the code skips it and keeps the overlap-only crop.

7. Three plates I picked

Downloaded from the Library of Congress as the same tall grey TIFFs and run through the same code, nothing changed. I picked them so the gallery would have something other than churches.

One that failed: Lake

Neither version aligned it. Raw NCC: G (−95, 239), R (−39, 265). Sobel: G (149, −27), R (−255, 118). Different answers in different directions, both visibly wrong. I don't have a confident explanation. The values sit near the limit the ±15 search at 1/16 scale can express (about ±240 px), so my guess is the coarsest level locked onto something that isn't the scene, but I haven't verified that.
